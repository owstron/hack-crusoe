## **Hackathon Problem Statements**

### **Done Means Done**

Agents fill in vague goals with their own assumptions, then confidently report success. Build an agent team on Crusoe Managed Inference that writes its own definition of done, checks its work with tests or independent verifier agents, catches itself drifting or looping, and decides whether to continue, re-plan, stop or (rarely) ping a human. How can agents turn a one-line goal into checks they can run without you, so you can actually walk away?

### **Shared Brain**

Every new agent on a team starts from zero and pays again for the same context. Build a shared memory layer for an agent team on Crusoe Managed Inference: a living project that every agent reads before it works and updates afterward. How can a team keep its shared knowledge current, lean and conflict-free while showing measurable gains in output quality?

### **Version N+1**

Most agent teams are exactly as good on run 100 as on run 1. Build an agent team on Crusoe Managed Inference that improves itself over time: after each run it reviews what worked and what didn’t, then rewrites its own prompts, playbooks, tools, or team structure, and keeps a change only if it measurably helps. How can a team turn its own history into a better version of itself, and prove it with a before-and-after score on tasks it hasn’t seen?

## **Judging Process**

Judging will take place in two rounds.

**Round One:** Hackers will be assigned to judging groups in different rooms of the venue. Each team will have \~3 minutes to live demo their project, followed by 1-2 minutes of Q&A. The following criteria will be used:

- **Technicality (40%):** How difficult is this project to recreate? Does this involve technicality beyond your average hackathon project? Are you impressed with what they built in such a short time?
- **Live Demo (20%):** How is the actual demo of the project? Does it impress you? Is it well-engineered and working?
- **Creativity and Originality (25%):** How creative is the project? Is this something you have never seen before?
- **Future Potential and AI Impact (15%):** Does this project point toward an interesting future for AI? Does it advance how we think about AI capabilities, autonomy, learning, evaluation, or agentic systems?

**Round Two:** The top six teams demoing on stage in front of a panel of judges and all attendees. Each team will have \~3 minutes to live demo their project, followed by 1-2 minutes of Q&A. The same criteria as above will be used, though with equal weighting for each category.

## **Hackathon Rules**

- **Open Source:** Repositories **must be public**.
- **Team Size:** A **maximum of four** team members per team. Solo participants are allowed.
- **Demo Requirements:** Your demo **must only highlight the specific features, code, and functionality that your team built during the hackathon**. Judges must be able to clearly identify what was created during the event. Failure to clearly identify your original contributions will result in immediate disqualification.
- **New Work Only:** You may not present an existing project as your own work. Failure to clearly distinguish your contributions will result in immediate disqualification.
- **Banned Projects:** Projects will be **disqualified** if they: violate legal, ethical, or platform policies, use code, data, or assets you do not have the rights to.
